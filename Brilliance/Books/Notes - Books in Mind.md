> Here's a few things that have been impactful for me. Based on where I am in my life, I unfortunatley don't have as much time to self-study and build my skills as I did in the past. I really miss those times, and they will probably return at another phase of my life. Also, sorry, I got lazy about including names of authors but all of these you can find on Amazon, etc.
> Hard Skills books that I frequently return to. They all have a similar quality, in that you can crack them open and spend a few hours on a chapter, and not necessarily have to work through them linearly.
> - Financial Modeling, Simon Benninga
> - Principles of Finance with Excel, Simon Benninga
> - Financial Planning & Analysis and Performance Management, Jack Alexander
> - Lessons in Corporate Finance, Paul Asquith and Lawrence Weiss
>   
For softer skills, I've never found anything that I would consider canonical but a few things taht I have liked include:
> - Learning as a Way of Being (hard to find a copy but you might find used ones on Amazon)
> - The Fifth Discipline (good for managers)
> - Strategy Beyond the Hockeystick (somewhere between hard and soft skills)
> - Anything by or about Michael Porter. Starting with Understanding Michael Porter by Joan Magretta
> - Crucial Influence
> - Difficult Conversations
> - Maxims for Thinking Analytically
> - Good Strategy, Bad Strategy
> - Assertiveness: How to Stand up for Yourself, Judy Murphy
> - The Courage to Be Disliked (this is more of a life self-help book but helped me a lot with all aspects of my life. I couldn't leave it out).
>   
>   For career management, there are a few books I like but with caveats per below:
> - 48 Laws of Power. There are few books that I feel like have an aura but this is one of them. This is a dangerous book. I feel like it should be kept under lock and key. I literally don't keep it on my bookshelf at home and have it sitting on a shelf in my closet. If you spend too much time absorbing its values and message, it may not serve you well and may hurt you. But taken with a grain of salt, and using its tactics carefully, or using it to understand the tactics of others, is incredibly powerful.
> - These next two books are also not books that I would recommend using as a guide for how to manage your career, but I think they provide useful ideas, and also ground you in certain realities of what really matters to your company and your boss.
> - Secrets to Winning at Office Politics,
> - The Organizational Politics Playbook
