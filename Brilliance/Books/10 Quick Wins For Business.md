# QUICK WIN #3: BUILD A MARKETING FUNNEL

1. Awareness (TOFU - Top of the Funnel):
- Goal: Make potential customers aware of your brand and what you offer.
- Activities: Content marketing, social media marketing, search engine optimization (SEO), public relations.
- Metrics: Website traffic, social media reach, brand mentions.

2. Interest (MOFU - Middle of the Funnel):
- Goal: Generate interest in your product or service and educate potential customers about its benefits.
- Activities: Blog posts, webinars, ebooks, white papers, case studies, and lead magnets (free resources in exchange for contact information).
- Metrics: Lead capture rates, conversion rates (e.g., email signups), time spent on content.

3. Decision (BOFU - Bottom of the Funnel):
- Goal: Nurture leads and convince them your product or service is the best solution for their needs.
- Activities: Email marketing, product demos, free trials, personalized offers, customer testimonials.
- Metrics: Sales conversions, customer lifetime value, churn rate (customers who stop using your product or service).

The AIDA Hierarchy:
1. Attention: The first hurdle is grabbing customers' attention in a crowded marketplace. This requires creative disruption – breaking away from established patterns with innovative messaging. Techniques like unexpected ad placements (guerilla marketing), surprising visuals, or highly targeted communication can all be effective.
2. Interest: Once you've captured attention, the challenge is to spark genuine interest in your product or service. Clear, concise messaging with eye-catching visuals is key. Focus on communicating the most relevant benefits to your target audience.
3. Desire: Having piqued interest, you need to move the customer from "like" to "want." This stage builds on the previous one, emphasizing why the customer actually needs your product. Think of infomercials that showcase the product's features and benefits in compelling ways. Explain how your offering solves their problems or improves their lives.
4. Action: The ultimate goal is to prompt a purchase decision. A clear call to action (CTA) is crucial here. Persuasive messaging, limited-time offers, or special promotions can incentivize action and create a sense of urgency.

**AAARRR** is a powerful tool for assessing and optimizing a business's development and performance.

- **Awareness**: Metrics like website traffic, social media reach, and brand mentions help you gauge how well your target audience is seeing your brand.
- **Acquisition**: Metrics like lead capture rates, conversion rates, and cost per acquisition show how effectively you convert website visitors and social media followers into potential customers.
- **Activation**: Activation metrics like product sign-ups, time spent using the product, and feature adoption rates tell you if new customers successfully engage with your product or service.
- **Retention**: Refers to keeping users engaged with your product or service and preventing them from churning (stopping use). Metrics tracked at this stage might include customer retention rate, churn rate, customer satisfaction score, and engagement rate.
- **Revenue**: Revenue metrics like average order value, customer lifetime value, and churn rate show you the financial health of your business and how effectively you're monetizing your customer base.
- **Referral**: Referral metrics like social media shares, NPS scores (Net Promoter Score), and referral program engagement indicate how happy your customers are and whether they recommend your business to others.

## Case Study: Utilizing the Pirate Metrics (AARRR) Framework for IKEA's Business Growth and Performance

This case study examines how IKEA can leverage Pirate Metrics effectively.

1. Awareness: Drawing Attention to Your Brand
- Objective: Increase brand visibility and attract potential customers.
- Strategy: IKEA uses traditional advertising, social media campaigns, and content marketing to build brand awareness.
- Execution: IKEA's extensive use of TV commercials, billboards, and online ads ensures that their brand remains top of mind for consumers. Their social media presence engages a broad audience with creative posts and videos.
Example:
- TV Commercials: IKEA runs impactful TV ads showcasing innovative products and solutions for everyday living.
- Social Media Campaigns: On platforms like Instagram and Facebook, IKEA shares visually appealing content, including room makeovers and DIY projects using their products, generating significant user engagement.

2. Acquisition: Converting Visitors into Customers
- Objective: Transform interested individuals into paying customers.
- Strategy: Simplify the shopping experience and offer incentives to encourage purchases.
- Execution: IKEA's website and stores are designed for ease of navigation. They offer promotions and discounts to new customers, making the first purchase attractive.
Example:
- Website Usability: The IKEA website features a user- friendly interface with intuitive product search and detailed information.
- Promotional Offers: New customers receive a discount code for their first purchase when they sign up for IKEA’s newsletter, encouraging them to make an initial purchase.

3. Activation: Ensuring a Positive Initial Experience
- Objective: Ensure the initial interaction with IKEA’s products or services is positive.
- Strategy: Provide an outstanding first experience with IKEA’s products and services, ensuring customers feel valued and delighted.
- Execution: In-store, IKEA offers a memorable shopping experience with clear product displays, helpful staff, and engaging product demonstrations. Online, they provide detailed assembly instructions and customer support.
Example:
- In-Store Experience: IKEA’s stores are designed to guide customers through fully furnished model rooms, showcasing products in real-life settings.
- Assembly Support: Every product comes with easy-to- follow assembly instructions, and customers have access to online video tutorials and live chat support.

4. Retention: Keeping Customers Engaged
- Objective: Maintain customer engagement and reduce churn.
- Strategy: Offer ongoing value through excellent customer service, regular updates, and exclusive content.
- Execution: IKEA engages customers with regular email newsletters featuring home decor tips, product news, and exclusive offers. Their loyalty program, IKEA Family, offers members special discounts and events.
Example:
- Email Newsletters: Subscribers receive monthly emails with design inspiration, new product announcements, and exclusive discounts.
- IKEA Family Program: Members get special discounts, invitations to exclusive events, and free coffee in the IKEA restaurant, enhancing the sense of community and loyalty.

5. Revenue: Maximizing Monetization Strategies
- Objective: Optimize revenue generation from customers.
- Strategy: Introduce tiered product lines, upsell additional products, and offer membership benefits.
- Execution: IKEA offers a wide range of products, from basic to premium lines, catering to different customer segments. They also promote complementary products, increasing the average order value.
Example:
- Tiered Product Lines: IKEA offers various product ranges, such as the budget-friendly "LACK" series and the more premium "BILLY" bookcases, appealing to diverse customer budgets.
- Cross-selling: When a customer buys a sofa, IKEA suggests matching cushions, throws, and coffee tables, encouraging additional purchases.

6. Referral: Encouraging Word-of-mouth Marketing
- Objective: Encourage satisfied customers to recommend IKEA to others.
- Strategy: Implement a referral program that rewards customers for bringing in new shoppers.
- Execution: IKEA offers referral incentives, such as discounts or vouchers for both the referrer and the new customer, motivating customers to spread the word.
Example:
Referral Program: Customers who refer friends to join the IKEA Family receive a discount on their next purchase, while the referred friend also gets a welcome discount, creating a mutually beneficial incentive.


**AWARENESS METRICS**

- *Impressions* - The number of times your content is displayed, regardless of clicks
- *Reach* - The number of unique users who have seen your content
- *Website traffic* - The number of visitors to your website is often broken down by new and returning visitors
- *Social media engagement* - Interactions on your social media posts (likes, shares, comments, followers)
- *Brand mentions* - The number of times your brand is mentioned online (social media, blogs, news articles, forums)
- *Search Volume Data* - The frequency with which your brand or related keywords are searched for on search engines.
- *Ad Recall Lift* - Measures how many people remember seeing your ad within a certain period.
- *Event Attendance* - The number of people attending your brand's online (webinars, live streams) and offline (store openings, product launches) events.

**ACTIVATION METRICS**

- *Sign-up Rate* - Percentage of visitors who sign up for an account, newsletter, or service
- *Onboarding Completion Rate* - Percentage of users who complete the onboarding process after signing up.
- *First Purchase or Conversion Rate* - Percentage of users who make their first purchase or complete a key action.
- *App Download and Usage Rate* - Number of downloads and frequency of mobile app use.
- *Engagement with Key Features* - Frequency of user interaction with important features.
- *Time to First Key Action* - Time taken for new users to complete a significant action (purchase, setting up profile).
- *Customer Feedback and NPS (Net Promotor Score)* - Direct user feedback about their initial experience (surveys).
- *Tutorial and Help Center Usage* - Number of users who utilize tutorials, help centers, or customer support during initial interactions.

**ACQUISITION METRICS**

- *Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)* - Total cost to acquire a new customer (marketing & sales expenses).
- *Conversion Rate* - Percentage of visitors taking a desired action (purchase, sign-up).
- *Click-through Rate (CTR)* - Percentage of people clicking on your ads or links (out of the total who view them).
- *Website Traffic* - Number of visitors to your website over a specific period.
- *Trial Sign-ups* - Number of users who sign up for a free trial of your product or service.
- *Bounce Rate* - Percentage of visitors who leave your website after viewing only one page.

**REVENUE METRICS**

- *Total Revenue* - The total amount of money generated from sales over a specific period.
- *Average Revenue Per User (ARPU)* - Average revenue generated per user/customer.
- *Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)* - Total revenue expected from a single customer account over their relationship.
- *Gross Margin* - The difference between revenue and cost of goods sold (COGS) is expressed as a percentage of revenue
- *Net Profit Margin* - Percentage of revenue remaining after all expenses, taxes, and costs are deducted.
- *Recurring Revenue* - The portion of revenue expected to continue in the future (subscriptions, repeat customers).
- *Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR)* - Predictable revenue expected every month from subscriptions or regular sales.

**RETENTION METRICS**

- *Customer Retention Rate (CRR)* - Percentage of customers retained over a given period.
$$
(Customers[end] - Customers[new]) / Customers[start]
$$
- *Churn Rate* - Percentage of customers who stop doing business in a given period.
$$
LostCustomers / Customers[start]
$$
- *Repeat Purchase Rate (RPR)* - Percentage of customers who make more than one purchase over a certain period.
- *Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)* - Total revenue expected from a single customer over their entire relationship.
$$ CLV(Estimate) = RevenuePerCustomer(avg) * CustomerLifespan(avg) $$
- *Net Promoter Score (NPS)* - Measure of customer satisfaction and loyalty (likelihood to recommend).
- *Customer Satisfaction Score (CSAT)* - Measure of customer satisfaction with a specific interaction or overall experience.
- *Average Order Value (AOV)* - Average amount spent per customer order.
$$ AOV = TR / NumberOfOrders $$
## THE RACE MODEL: A ROADMAP FOR EFFECTIVE DIGITAL MARKETING

1. **Reach (R)**: The focus is building brand awareness and increasing online visibility. "Owned" (your website and social media pages), "Bought" (paid advertising), "Earned" (mentions and reviews) media.
2. **Act (A)**: Now that you've attracted visitors, the Act stage focuses on encouraging them to interact with your brand. Attention -> Action (Subscribe, SignUp etc.)
3. **Convert (C)**: This stage is about driving conversions, whether online purchases or offline actions like generating valuable sales leads.
4. **Engage (E)**: Building long-term customer relationship is key to sustainable success. The Engage stage focuses on fostering customer loyalty and encouraging repeat business.

# QUICK WIN #9: TRACK KEY METRICS (KPI'S)

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) provide invaluable insights into your business's health and progress.

**SALES KPIs**

1. Average Order Value (AOV) - average monetary value of each customer's purchase

$$AOV = Total \space Revenue \space Number / \space of \space Orders$$

2. Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) - total revenue a business can expect from a single customer over their entire relationship with the company.

$$ CLV = Average \space Purchase \space Value \times Purchase \space Frequency \times Average \space Retention \space Time$$

**MARKETING KPIS**

1. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) - expense incurred to acquire a new customer.

$$ CAC = Total \space Marketing \space and \space Sales \space Costs / Number \space of \space New \space Customers $$

2. Landing Page Conversion Rates - measure the percentage of visitors to a specific landing page who take a desired action, such as filling out a form, signing up, or making a purchase.

LPCR = Number of Conversions / Number of Visitors
